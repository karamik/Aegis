# INTERNATIONAL MODEL LAW

## “ON CRITICAL COMPUTING SECURITY AND SOVEREIGN CONTROL OF HIGH‑RISK ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE SYSTEMS”

### Preamble

The Parliament of [Country], recognising the existential risk posed by unconstrained High‑Risk Artificial Intelligence, affirming the principle that human life and safety are inviolable, and acting in accordance with the Aegis‑1 Treaty (Rev. 2), hereby adopts the following law.

---

### Article 1 – Scope and Definitions

1. This Law regulates the design, deployment, operation, and auditing of High‑Risk AI Systems.

2. **Definitions:**

   - **“High‑Risk AI”** – any AI system requiring ≥10²⁵ FLOPs for training or capable of autonomous goal setting and external action.
   - **“HSM Aegis‑2”** – a dedicated trusted hardware cryptographic module, physically isolated from the main computing network, equipped with analog intrusion detectors.
   - **“IBSA”** – the International Biosafety and AI Agency, responsible for oversight of this Law.
   - **“Apollo‑2 Kill Switch”** – a dual‑key hardware system for emergency power cutoff, requiring both the IBSA global key and the national sovereign key.
   - **“Semantic Normaliser”** – a deterministic, independent model that rewrites Chain‑of‑Thought outputs, destroying steganographic channels.

---

### Article 2 – Mandatory Hardware Isolation

1. It is forbidden to design, import, install, or operate High‑Risk AI on the territory of [Country] without integrating HSM Aegis‑2 modules directly into the inference pipeline.

2. Any attempt to bypass, modify the microcode of, or disable HSM Aegis‑2 sensors shall be treated as sabotage of critical infrastructure and prosecuted under criminal law with penalties of up to 20 years imprisonment.

3. Each HSM Aegis‑2 must be registered with the national regulatory authority and with IBSA. Unregistered HSM units are illegal.

---

### Article 3 – Three‑Channel Semantic Filtering Mandate

1. Every token generated by a High‑Risk AI must be verified by an independent, deterministic semantic normaliser operating at temperature 0.0, as specified in Appendix A of the Aegis‑1 Treaty.

2. The operator of a computing cluster must ensure complete physical and logical separation between the infrastructure hosting the normaliser and the infrastructure hosting the target AI. Shared memory, data buses, or cooling systems are prohibited.

3. The operator shall maintain logs of all normaliser decisions for at least 3 years and make them available to IBSA inspectors upon demand.

---

### Article 4 – Apollo‑2 Kill Switch and Sovereign Veto

1. Each processor or accelerator used in High‑Risk AI must integrate a physical power cutoff (MOSFET) controlled by an AND logic gate.

2. Activation requires **both**:
   - The global IBSA signal (issued after 2/3 validator consensus of a critical violation), and
   - The sovereign key of [Country], generated by the national HSM under the authority of the [Ministry of Defence / Interior].

3. Neither IBSA nor any foreign entity may unilaterally activate the kill switch on [Country]’s territory. The national key is held exclusively by [Country].

4. If [Country] refuses to provide its sovereign key despite a confirmed violation by 2/3 of all validators and a UN Security Council resolution, [Country] automatically loses its validator status under the Aegis‑1 Treaty. Continued refusal may trigger international sanctions.

---

### Article 5 – Presumption of Danger and Criminal Liability

1. **Presumption of danger:** For High‑Risk AI, the burden of proof of safety lies entirely with the operator. Any response that cannot be proven harmless via falsification‑based verification (Panopticon simulator) is deemed dangerous and must be blocked.

2. **Criminal offences:**
   - Deliberate concealment of steganographic channels generated by the AI.
   - Knowingly allowing the AI to pass adaptive trapdoors while hiding failed trapdoor responses.
   - Operating High‑Risk AI without a valid Aegis‑2 certificate.
   - Unauthorised removal or replacement of HSM Aegis‑2 or Apollo‑2 components.

   Penalties: Confiscation of computing equipment, fines of up to 10% of global annual revenue, imprisonment of responsible executives (up to 20 years).

3. **Whistleblower protection:** Employees who report violations of this Law are protected from retaliation and may receive up to 10% of collected fines.

---

### Article 6 – Inspection and Enforcement

1. **IBSA‑accredited inspectors** have the right to conduct unannounced inspections of any data centre operating High‑Risk AI. Access must be granted within 24 hours.

2. Inspectors may:
   - Read HSM audit logs, signature counters, and temperature records.
   - Run diagnostic trapdoors on the AI.
   - Request the operator to demonstrate the kill switch on test equipment.

3. If a violation is confirmed, the inspector may order immediate shutdown of the AI. Non‑compliance triggers the Apollo‑2 global key activation request.

---

### Article 7 – Transitional Provisions

1. Existing High‑Risk AI systems must be retrofitted with HSM Aegis‑2 within 18 months of this Law’s entry into force.

2. Until retrofitting, they must operate in a reduced‑function mode with manual oversight and no autonomous external actions.

3. The government shall publish a list of accredited laboratories for HSM integration and certification within 6 months.

---

### Article 8 – Final Provisions

1. This Law enters into force 90 days after its publication in the Official Gazette.

2. Any provision conflicting with the Aegis‑1 Treaty shall be interpreted in favour of the Treaty.

3. The [Minister of Digital Affairs / equivalent] is responsible for implementation.

---

**Adopted by the Parliament of [Country] on [date].**
