# Code of Conduct for Aegis‑1 Contributors

## Our Pledge

We as members, contributors, and leaders of the Aegis‑1 project pledge to make participation in our community a harassment‑free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio‑economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive, and **secure** community.

## Our Standards

Examples of behaviour that contributes to a positive environment:
- Using respectful language
- Accepting constructive criticism
- Focusing on what is best for the safety of the protocol

**Unacceptable behaviour:**
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, personal or political attacks
- Public or private harassment
- Publishing others’ private information without explicit permission
- **Deliberately introducing security vulnerabilities** (this will be treated as a violation of the Aegis‑1 Treaty and may be prosecuted)
- Attempting to bypass the semantic normaliser or HSM controls in submitted code

## Enforcement Responsibilities

Project maintainers (IBSA Technical Committee) are responsible for clarifying and enforcing this Code of Conduct. They have the right to remove, edit, or reject comments, commits, code, wiki edits, issues, and other contributions that are not aligned with this Code of Conduct.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all project spaces (GitHub, mailing lists, official meetings) and also when an individual is representing the project in public spaces.

## Enforcement

Instances of abusive, harassing, or otherwise unacceptable behaviour may be reported to the project team at `conduct@ibsa.aegis`. All complaints will be reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly.

All project maintainers are obligated to respect the privacy and security of the reporter.

## Consequences of Unacceptable Behaviour

Unacceptable behaviour may result in:
- Temporary or permanent ban from the repository
- Revocation of contributor status
- Notification to IBSA for possible legal action under the Aegis‑1 Treaty
